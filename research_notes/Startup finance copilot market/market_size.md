# Market size and demand: finance / FP&A / cash-management software for early-stage startups (seed to Series A, 2–50 people)

Research date: November 2026. Scope: US first, then UK/EU and India. All figures carry their year. "Inference" means my own arithmetic or reasoning, not a sourced fact. Sources include several low-quality market-research aggregators. Each one is flagged.

## 1. How many startups are at pre-seed, seed and Series A, and how is this changing (2024–2026)?

### Takeaway
US seed *deal counts* are falling (Carta: −21% YoY in Q1 2026; Crunchbase: rounds of $200K to <$5M down about 20% in 2025). *Dollars* are rising and concentrating in AI, and teams are much smaller (median seed team about 4, average Series A team about 14). The US starts roughly 5,000–6,000 newly funded companies a year (PitchBook: about 6,000 first financings in 2025). That implies a standing US seed-to-Series-A population in the low tens of thousands, and every one of them is a 2–50-person company.

### Cited Findings
**US / North America deal flow**
- Carta Q1 2026: seed startups raised $2.7B across **381 rounds**, vs $2.2B across 481 rounds in Q1 2025. Deal count fell 21% and capital rose 25%. Carta counts only companies on its cap-table platform, so this is a sample, not the whole market, and Carta notes that recent quarters are revised up because of reporting lag. — [Carta State of Private Markets Q1 2026](https://carta.com/data/state-of-private-markets-q1-2026-full-report/) (the page returned 403 when fetched; figures come from the search-result summary)
- A secondary summary of earlier Carta data puts Q1 2025 at 401 seed rounds, which conflicts with the 481 in Carta's Q1 2026 report (lag revisions). — [blog.mean.ceo](https://blog.mean.ceo/?p=5728)
- Crunchbase 2025, US + Canada: **$280B** across seed-to-growth rounds (+46% YoY); deal count fell about 16% to **"just under 10,500 reported rounds"**; seed was **about $20.4B**; Series A+B was close to $69B (+5%); Q4 2025 alone had "just over **1,300** reported seed financings"; AI took about 60% of all North American funding ($168B). — [Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/north-american-startup-funding-2025-data-ai-us-investment/)
- Crunchbase: US seed funding was $19.4B in 2025. Rounds of $10M+ made up 51% of seed dollars. Deal counts and amounts for $200K to <$5M rounds fell about 20% YoY. Rounds of ≤$5M fell from 93% of seed deal count (2018) to 75% (2025). — [Pipelineroad summary of Crunchbase data](https://pipelineroad.com/news/20260326-seed-funding-in-u-s-skewing-larger-in-2025-crunchbase-data-s); [Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/seed-funding-isnt-small-anymore-ai-start-ups-push-rounds-historic-highs)
- PitchBook-NVCA (Q4 2025 Venture Monitor coverage): about **16,700 US VC deals in 2025** (16,707, +9.6% YoY). The early-stage deal count was "roughly on par" with 2023–2024. 2025 was on track for **nearly 6,000 first financings**, a record. PitchBook's count is much higher than Crunchbase's 10,500 because PitchBook's methodology includes angel deals, small deals and lagged deals. — [Fortune on PitchBook 2025](https://fortune.com/2026/01/16/2025-us-vc-deal-value-soared-pitchbook-but-theres-a-catch/?rand=8593); [GamesBeat Q4 2025](https://gamesbeat.com/?p=314479); [Q4 2025 Venture Monitor PDF](https://nvca.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/q4-2025-pitchbook-nvca-venture-monitor.pdf) (the PDF was too large to fetch, so this could not be checked against the original)
- A secondary source citing PitchBook says seed deal count fell 29% YoY while median valuations rose 19% (not verified). — [stealthagents](https://stealthagents.com/research/startup-fundraising-statistics-2026)
- PitchBook-NVCA Q1 2026: AI startups took 38.9% of deal count, and Q1 2026 deal value hit a record $267.2B, driven by a handful of mega AI deals. — [GamesBeat Q1 2026](https://gamesbeat.com/ai-fundings-double-vc-deal-value-to-267-2b-in-q1-thanks-to-openai-and-others-pitchbook-nvca/); [NVCA](https://nvca.org/pitchbook-nvca-venture-monitor)

**Global**
- Crunchbase: $425B of global venture and growth funding went to more than 24,000 private companies in 2025. Over 42% of global seed dollars went to AI (30% in 2024), more than $15B. — [techround](https://techround.co.uk/funding/global-startup-funding-2025); [Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/?p=93083)
- CB Insights (secondary citation): $469B across **29,501 global deals in 2025**, deal count −17%. — [stealthagents](https://stealthagents.com/research/startup-fundraising-statistics-2026)

**Accelerators**
- YC moved to four batches a year in 2025, with roughly 140–200 companies per batch and about 626 companies in 2025 (aggregator figure, not official YC). W26 had 196–199 companies. Spring 2026 is reported as both 80 and 193 (unresolved). — [wearefounders](https://www.wearefounders.uk/y-combinator-acceptance-rate-2026/); [extruct W26](https://www.extruct.ai/research/ycw26); [roundfunded](https://www.roundfunded.com/blogs/yc-batches-2026-dates-acceptance-rate)

**Team size (supports the 2–50 headcount target)**
- Carta H2 2025: median seed team is **4 employees**. H1 2025: the average seed team was 6.2, down from 11.1 in 2021 (−44%). Average Series A team size was down 37% vs 2021. — [Carta comp H2 2025](https://carta.com/sg/en/data/startup-compensation-h2-2025-full-report/); [Carta comp H1 2025](https://carta.com/data/startup-compensation-h1-2025-full-report/)
- Carta: the average Series A startup raising in 2025 had **about 14 FTEs** (22 in 2022). — [Carta "AI takes startup jobs?"](https://carta.com/data/newsletter-ai-takes-startup-jobs/); [Carta headcount freeze](https://carta.com/data/linkedin-startup-headcount-frozen-2025/)

### Inferences
- **US newly funded companies per year:** about 5,000–6,000. Anchors: PitchBook's ~6,000 first financings and Crunchbase's ~1,300 seed rounds in Q4 alone (about 5,000+ a year).
- **US standing population (stock) at seed to Series A:** a seed company typically spends about 2–3 years before raising an A or failing, so the seed stock is about 12,000–18,000. Adding Series A companies (≈2,000–3,000 A rounds a year × ~2 years ≈ 4,000–6,000) gives a **core venture-backed US base of ~16,000–24,000**. Adding pre-seed, angel and accelerator-only companies plausibly doubles it to **~35,000–50,000**. These dwell times are my assumptions and need validation (for example against Carta's count of active companies).
- **Global:** US deal count is roughly half of global count when PitchBook-US (~16.7K) is compared with CB Insights-global (~29.5K). The methodologies differ, so this is a rough ratio. Global core seed-to-A stock is therefore ~2× the US: **~35,000–50,000 core** and ~70,000–100,000 including pre-seed.
- **Demand-side trend:** fewer but larger seed rounds and smaller teams mean fewer finance hires per company. That favours software (a co-pilot) over people, so demand per company may rise even as the company count falls modestly.

### Gaps
- No primary count of *active* US venture-backed companies was found (PitchBook and Carta publish these figures, but they sit behind paywalls or could not be fetched).
- No full-year 2025 US seed *deal count* was found from Crunchbase, PitchBook or Carta directly. Q3 2026 Venture Monitor deal counts were also not found.
- YC's official batch counts were not found. The aggregators conflict.

### Inferences
- Intuit's QBO revenue alone (~$5B) is at or above most analysts' entire "SMB accounting software" estimates, which shows how unreliable those reports are. Primary filings should anchor the model.
- Implied QBO revenue per online customer is about $5,051M ÷ ~8.9M ≈ $570/year. This is rough because the customer base includes Mailchimp and similar products. Xero's ARPU is NZ$55.44/month ≈ NZ$665/year. The SMB core-ledger price anchor is therefore **about $500–700/year**. A co-pilot layered on top has to justify a price above or near that.
- Rising ARPU (+15% at Intuit, +23% reported at Xero) shows SMBs will absorb price increases for AI and finance features. This supports demand for paid add-on finance intelligence.

### Gaps
- No Gartner or IDC figures were found (paywalled). There is also no analyst estimate specific to "finance automation for startups".
- Methodologies are not disclosed for most report-mill figures.

## 3. How much do early-stage startups spend on finance tooling and people?

### Takeaway
A seed to Series A startup typically spends **~$600–1,200/month on outsourced bookkeeping** and **~$3,000–10,000/month on a fractional CFO** once it has one, plus **$0–2,000/month on FP&A/runway software**. A total finance stack of **~$1K–$10K+/month** leaves room for a $100–500/month co-pilot, especially one that replaces or postpones the fractional CFO or the FP&A tool.

### Cited Findings
- Kruze (startup-specialist accounting firm): bookkeeping starts at **$600/month**. Basic tier (<$150K monthly expenses, "great for pre-revenue, seed-stage") is $650–750/month. Founder Timesaver is $850–1,200/month. — [Kruze pricing](https://kruzeconsulting.com/pricing/); [Kruze bookkeeping](https://kruzeconsulting.com/bookkeeping-for-vc-backed-startups)
- Pilot: the figures conflict. Pilot's own blog lists Essentials $199, Core $599 and Custom. Burkland (Aug 2026) cites $99/month Essentials and Pilot CFO services from $1,750/month. — [Pilot blog](https://pilot.com/blog/kruze-consulting-alternatives); [Burkland](https://burklandassociates.com/?p=12292)
- Fractional CFO: US retainers run $3,000–15,000/month, most commonly $5,000–10,000 for startups and small businesses. Startups with $500K–2M revenue pay $3,500–5,000/month for 10–20 hours. Early-stage startups may need 8–10 hours a month. Hourly rates are $175–500. Most of these figures come from vendor blogs, so treat them as marketing benchmarks. — [Pilot fractional CFO guide](https://pilot.com/blog/fractional-cfo-cost-guide); [Zeni](https://www.zeni.ai/blog/how-much-does-a-fractional-cfo-cost); [Forecastr](https://www.forecastr.co/blog/fractional-cfo-startups); [Kruze CFO charges](https://kruzeconsulting.com/blog/startup-cfo-charges/)
- FP&A and runway tools (review-blog figures, which vary widely):
  - Runway: about $149–299/month, with some sources quoting $500–2,000.
  - Mosaic: $1,000–3,000+/month, about $24K/year plus a $10–25K implementation fee.
  - Jirav: Starter $10K/year, Pro $15K/year.
  - Puzzle: free, $50 or $100/month tiers.
  - Pry (now part of Brex): from $12/user/month.
  — [eightx Jirav](https://eightx.co/blog/compare/how-much-does-jirav-cost); [eightx Mosaic/Jirav](https://eightx.co/blog/fpa-tool-worth-it-3m-mosaic-jirav); [ValueAdd VC](https://valueaddvc.com/blog/best-financial-modeling-tools-for-startups-excel-vs-runway-vs-causal-vs-mosaic); [modelmonkey](https://modelmonkey.io/blog/platforms-tracking-runway-burn-rate)
- Ledger anchor: Xero ARPU is about NZ$55/month and QBO is roughly $570/customer/year (see Section 2).

### Inferences
- Typical seed-stage stack: ledger ($30–100/month) + outsourced bookkeeping ($600–1,200) + spreadsheets or a cheap runway tool ($0–300) ≈ **$700–1,600/month**.
- Typical Series A stack: the seed stack + a fractional CFO ($3–8K) + an FP&A tool ($500–2,000) ≈ **$4–11K/month**.
- A co-pilot priced at **$100–500/month** is 5–30% of that finance wallet. Pricing toward the Runway/Jirav band ($150–1,250/month) is defensible for Series A.

### Gaps
- There is no independent survey of startup finance spend by stage; all figures come from vendor pricing pages and marketing blogs.
- The share of seed companies that actually pay for an FP&A tool (vs using spreadsheets) is unknown.

## 4. Bottom-up TAM/SAM/SOM model with ranges and adjacencies (including geography)

### Takeaway
The pure venture-backed seed-to-A segment is a **niche: US TAM ~$70M–$300M a year, global ~$150M–$600M**. The market becomes meaningfully larger (low billions) only by expanding to cloud-ledger SMBs (Intuit's ~8.9M and Xero's ~4.9M paying customers) and by selling multi-client seats to accounting and fractional-CFO firms (~46,000–52,000 US CPA firms). A realistic 5-year SOM is **~$2M–$10M ARR**.

### Cited Findings (inputs; the counts are from the sections above)
- US first financings are about 6,000 a year (PitchBook 2025); North American seed is about 1,300 rounds per quarter (Crunchbase Q4 2025). — see Section 1.
- The US has more than 36M small businesses (<500 employees, mostly nonemployers; based on 2022 Census data). — [SBA Office of Advocacy](https://advocacy.sba.gov/2025/06/30/2025-small-business-profiles-for-the-states-territories-and-nation/)
- About 46,000 CPA firms by most estimates (another estimate gives 52,200 firms; about 87,261 accounting firms including non-CPA firms; 2024 almanac). The US has 653,408 actively licensed CPAs (NASBA 2025). — [CPA Trendlines 2024](https://cpatrendlines.com/2024/01/01/cornerstone-cpa-trendlines-almanac-key-data-points-for-the-tax-accounting-and-finance-profession/); [ipassthecpaexam](https://ipassthecpaexam.com/number-of-cpa/)
- Intuit has about 8.9M online paying customers; Xero has 4.92M subscribers. — see Section 2.
- **UK:** 2025 seed funding is reported as $1.2B (−27%), which conflicts with another report of £1.5B (+19%). The UK had 534 fintech deals (all stages) in 2025. — [Fintech Times](https://thefintechtimes.com/uk-fintech-funding-rebounds-10-in-2025-as-investors-flock-to-late-stage-giants/); [Crowdfund Insider](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/2026/01/257311-private-securities-report-states-uk-venture-funding-shifts-to-later-stage-ranks-second-in-the-world/)
- **India:** tech startups raised $10.5B in 2025 (−17%). Seed was $1.1B (−30% vs 2024) per Tracxn. India ranks 3rd globally in funding. — [The Week / Tracxn](https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/business/2025/12/18/dcm91-biz-tech-startups-funding.html); [IANS](https://ianslive.in/india-tech-startups-rank-3rd-globally-in-funding-raise-105-billion-in-2025--20251218165716)
- **Europe:** Innovate Finance counted 1,391 European fintech deals (all stages) in 2025. No seed-only European count was found. — [Fintech Times](https://thefintechtimes.com/uk-fintech-funding-rebounds-10-in-2025-as-investors-flock-to-late-stage-giants/)

### Inferences: proposed bottom-up model (all assumptions are explicit)

**Segment A: venture-backed seed to Series A (core ICP)**

| | Low | Base | High |
|---|---|---|---|
| US companies (stock, see Section 1) | 16,000 | 25,000 | 50,000 (incl. pre-seed) |
| Blended annual price | $2,400 ($200/mo) | $4,200 ($350/mo) | $6,000 ($500/mo) |
| **US TAM** | **$38M** | **$105M** | **$300M** |
| Global multiplier (US ≈ 45–55% of count) | ×1.8 | ×2.0 | ×2.2 |
| **Global TAM** | **$70M** | **$210M** | **$660M** |

**Segment B: adjacent SMBs (bootstrapped SaaS, agencies, other 2–50-person service firms on cloud ledgers)**
- Universe: ~8.9M Intuit online customers + ~4.9M Xero subscribers. These overlap little, since Xero is mostly outside the US. Assume 10–20% are 2–50-person firms with recurring revenue or project billing that would care about runway and AR, i.e. about 1.4M–2.8M firms.
- Price: $50–150/month ($600–1,800/year), close to ledger ARPU.
- **TAM ≈ $0.8B–$5B** globally. This depends heavily on the qualifying share, which is an assumption.

**Segment C: accounting firms and fractional CFOs as multi-client buyers**
- 46,000–52,000 US CPA firms. Assume 10–20% run client advisory or startup practices, i.e. about 5,000–10,000 firms, each with about 10–40 relevant clients.
- Price: about $50–100 per client per month (wholesale).
- **US TAM ≈ $30M–$480M.** Ranges are wide; the client counts per firm are assumptions.

**SAM (what FinanceOps can actually serve with its planned integrations: Plaid (US-centric), Stripe, QuickBooks/Xero, Gusto/Rippling (US payroll), English-language)**
- US Segment A plus UK/EU English-speaking startups on Xero or QBO. Gusto and Rippling are mainly US, so the payroll integration limits non-US value. That gives **SAM ≈ $60M–$200M**: roughly the base US TAM plus about 25–50% for UK/EU.
- India: large seed volume but low willingness to pay (likely $30–100/month) and different rails (no Plaid; Tally and Zoho are dominant). This is my inference; I found no source on Indian startup accounting tool share. Treat India as a later market.

**SOM (3–5 years)**
- Capture 2–5% of US core startups (500–1,250 companies) at about $4,200/year, which gives **$2M–$5M ARR**.
- Add accounting-firm channel seats and UK/EU customers for an upside of **~$10M ARR**.
- Benchmark: Intuit and Xero show strong ARPU expansion, but no reliable customer counts were found for direct startup-FP&A competitors (Runway, Mosaic, Jirav), so SOM benchmarks against peers remain a gap.

**Key sensitivities**
1. Stock dwell time per stage (2 vs 3 years) moves the company count by about ±30%.
2. Price: a $200 vs $500 monthly price is a 2.5× swing.
3. Whether pre-seed and angel-funded companies are counted (about 2× the count).
4. The fall in seed deal counts (−20% to −29% in 2025–26) shrinks Segment A, while the AI boom and smaller teams increase the need for software over finance hires.

### Gaps
- No primary US count of employer firms with 2–49 employees was retrieved; the SBA's 36M figure is mostly nonemployers. The Census SUSB employer-firm breakdown should be pulled.
- No count of fractional-CFO practitioners or firms, and no "fractional CFO market size" figure, was found.
- No count of accounting firms offering startup or advisory services was found.
- No customer or ARR figures for direct competitors (Runway, Mosaic, Jirav, Puzzle, Pilot, Brex) were found to calibrate SOM.
- No comparable seed deal counts were found for the UK, EU and India (Dealroom data not accessible). Only funding totals are available, and the UK figures conflict.
- The global multiplier mixes PitchBook (US) and CB Insights (global) methodologies and is only approximate.
